## What changed from v8

v8 taught the router correctly and in the wrong **order**, which is the fault
S-04 exists to catch.

**1. It defined "expert" before showing one being used.** Beats 4 and 5 were
two definitions — *an expert is a block of numbers*, *nobody assigned them
subjects* — arriving before the viewer had watched a single score happen.
Sanderson's rule is the opposite: *"resist the temptation to open a topic by
describing a general result or definition."* The definition now lands at beat
11, after the eight have been picked.

**2. The misconception was pre-empted instead of corrected.** *"There's no
French expert"* was said before anyone was tempted to think there was. It now
arrives at beat 10, immediately after eight experts visibly get chosen — the
moment the viewer is most likely to assume the router picked the ones that
*know about dogs*. Headache before aspirin.

**3. The shared expert was a footnote.** v8's beat 10 literally began *"Oh —
and one extra"*. §1 defers it to this section precisely because this is where
it can be drawn, so it gets its own beat and its own place **outside** the wall
— it is not one of the 288.

**4. The payload had two beats and needed three.** *The scores were made from
numbers that only just existed* is what §11's whole answer rests on. It is now
Act 4, it is cited **S-15**, and it is the part of this video nobody else in
the field is making.

**5. Beat 13 was mis-cited as S-12.** S-12 names a mechanism at the close
*because it has not been named before* — but this section names the router at
beat 2, which it must, since eleven beats then talk about it. Beat 13 is a
chapter wall and nothing else, so it is **S-14**.

**5. Beat 15 promised a different eight.** A changed row means the router runs
again, not that all eight change — partial overlap between consecutive routing
decisions is real and measured, and §12's whole caching argument depends on it.
v9 made §7 and §12 contradict each other five sections apart. Beat 15 now says
*a fresh decision, and mostly a different eight*.
`research/glm/OFFLOADING_AND_LOCALITY.md` §5.

## The script

### Act 1 — banking the changed row (beat 1) · **S-14**

> **1.** *(the changed row travels right and settles at an unlabelled desk)* So
> the row belongs to this sentence now, not just to the word. Which means
> there's finally something worth reading — and something has to read it.

### Act 2 — the thing that chooses takes a name (beat 2) · **S-13**

> **2.** *(a small plaque lands on the desk)* This is the **router**. It's tiny
> compared to everything around it, and it has exactly one job.

### Act 3 — watch it work, then hear what it is (beats 3–12) · **S-04**

> **3.** *(we pull back; the 288 fill the frame beyond the desk)* In front of
> it: two hundred and eighty-eight experts. It has to pick some.
>
> **4.** *(the row arrives at the desk and stops; the wall waits)* The row
> arrives. And the router does exactly one thing with it.
>
> **5.** *(an empty score badge appears on all 288 at once)* It gives every
> single expert a score. All two hundred and eighty-eight of them.
>
> **6.** *(a sweep crosses the wall left to right, filling every badge)* And
> the question it's scoring is always the same one. How well does this expert
> fit **these** numbers?
>
> **7.** *(the badges settle; the row stays lit beside them)* Not this word. Not
> this sentence. These numbers, as they are right now.
>
> **8.** *(the eight highest rise forward; the other 280 go flat)* Then it keeps
> the best eight. That's it. That's the whole decision.
>
> **9.** *(a bracket draws around the eight and counts them against the wall)*
> Eight, out of two hundred and eighty-eight. The other two hundred and eighty
> do nothing at all.
>
> **10.** *(name-plates try to land on the eight and slide off)* Now — it's
> tempting to think it picked the eight that know about dogs. It didn't. There
> is no dog expert. There's no French expert, no maths expert.
>
> **11.** *(one expert lifts out and opens: a row goes in, a different row comes
> out)* They're two hundred and eighty-eight blocks of numbers that came out of
> training slightly different from each other. A row goes in, a different row
> comes out. That is the whole of what an expert is.
>
> **12.** *(a ninth slides in from outside the wall entirely and sits beside the
> eight, already lit)* And there's one more, over here. It isn't one of the two
> hundred and eighty-eight, and it never gets scored — it just runs, for every
> word, whatever the word is. So: eight picked, plus that one.

### Act 4 — where the scores came from (beats 13–15) · **S-15**

> **13.** *(the row pulses; a line links it to the badges)* But look at what
> those scores were made from. These numbers.
>
> **14.** *(attention's arcs from §4 ghost in behind the row, then fade)* The
> ones that didn't exist a moment ago. Attention had only just finished making
> them.
>
> **15.** *(a second sentence swaps in; the row changes; most of the eight
> change)* Change the sentence, and the row changes. Change the row, and the
> scores change. Change the scores, and it picks again — a fresh decision, and
> mostly a different eight.

Act 4 is the section's reason to exist, and it is the argument
`research/COMPETITIVE_FIELD.md` shows nobody else is making. Every competing
video explains top-k routing; none of them dwells on *when the choice becomes
knowable*. §11's entire answer is a consequence of these three beats.

### Act 5 — the wall (beat 16) · **S-14**

> **16.** *(the new eight hold; the old eight's empty places stay marked)* And
> that's the router, done. Eight picked out of two hundred and eighty-eight —
> and that choice could not have been made any earlier than this. It needed the
> row to exist first.

### Board notes

- **Two camera moves, both at the front**, and each earns a place change: to the
  desk, then out to the room. Beats 4–16 are still.
- **Beat 10 is the most important frame for not teaching a lie**, and in v8 it
  did not exist. The wall must carry no labels at any point, and beat 10 makes
  that a visible *event*: three name-plates — `dog`, `French`, `maths` — try to
  land on the chosen eight and slide off. A viewer who builds the "each expert
  is a subject" model will not unlearn it later, so the frame has to refuse the
  plates in front of them rather than the script quietly never offering one.
  `GROUND_TRUTH.md`: *"identity is a number."*
- **Beat 10 comes after beat 8, not before it.** That is the change. The
  temptation to think the router picked the dog-experts only exists once eight
  have visibly been chosen, so the correction lands there and not earlier.
- Beat 6's sweep must **visibly touch all 288.** If it only crosses the winners,
  the frame says the router looked at eight, which is the opposite of the point.
- **Beat 12 places the ninth expert outside the wall.** It is not one of the
  288 — `n_routed_experts` is 288 and `n_shared_experts` is 1 on top of it — so
  it must not be drawn as a member of the grid, or beat 9's *"the other two
  hundred and eighty"* becomes wrong by one. §1 defers it to here precisely
  because here it can be drawn.
- Beats 13–15 are the section's whole argument: the row, lines from it to the
  scores, and then the row's own source ghosting in behind it. **The choice
  comes from numbers that did not exist a moment earlier.** Beat 14 borrows §4's
  arcs deliberately — the viewer should recognise them.
- Beat 16 keeps the vacated positions marked, because a new eight lighting up
  does not read as *different* unless you can see where the old ones were. This
  is the seed §12 spends.

## Truth notes

- **Beat 5 is mandatory.** "The maths expert" is the single most common wrong
  model of MoE and it is very hard to unlearn. Identity is an index.
- GLM uses **sigmoid** expert scoring; the maths is deliberately not shown. Do
  not put score values on screen unless measured from activations.
- 288 routed + 1 shared, **top-8**. `GROUND_TRUTH.md`.
- The router reads the hidden state **after** attention. That ordering is the
  crux of the whole video and beat 11 says it out loud.

## Frames

- The router was on screen unlabelled from §2 beat 1. Naming it here is the
  payoff of that patience — **function before name**.
- The scoring sweep is `measure` (blue) across all 288, and it must visibly
  touch every one. If it only sweeps the winners, the frame lies.
- Chosen experts keep their family colour and gain an ink ring; the rest go
  flat beige. No new hue. `art-direction/CAST.md`.

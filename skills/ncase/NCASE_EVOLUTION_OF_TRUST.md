## 1. The opening — verbatim, and what it is doing

This is the entire intro screen, quoted in full because the structure is only
visible at full length:

> During World War I, peace broke out.
>
> It was Christmas 1914 on the Western Front. Despite strict orders not to
> chillax with the enemy, British and German soldiers left their trenches,
> crossed No Man's Land, and gathered to bury their dead, exchange gifts, and
> play games.
>
> Meanwhile: it's 2017, the West has been at peace for decades, and wow, we
> suck at trust. Surveys show that, over the past forty years, fewer and fewer
> people say they trust each other.
>
> So here's our puzzle: Why, even in peacetime, do friends become enemies? And
> why, even in wartime, do enemies become friends?
>
> I think game theory can help explain our epidemic of distrust – and how we
> can fix it! So, to understand all this...

Then the button: **"...let's play a game. →"**

Five moves, in this order:

| | move | what it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **"During World War I, peace broke out."** | A six-word sentence that is a contradiction on its own terms. No topic, no subject, no number. |
| 2 | the truce, concretely | Trenches, No Man's Land, burying the dead, gifts. Specific enough to picture. |
| 3 | **"Meanwhile: it's 2017…"** | **The inversion onto the reader.** The historical fact was the *setup*; the payload is *you are worse at this than soldiers in a trench were.* |
| 4 | the symmetrical question | Both halves of the paradox, asked together, so neither reads as an anecdote. |
| 5 | the promise, then the exit | *"can help explain… and how we can fix it"* — outcome stated plainly — then straight into concrete play. |

### The rule

> **Open on a concrete contradiction, then invert it onto the viewer's own
> present. The historical or external fact is the setup; the payload is that it
> implicates them. State the outcome before the first interaction.**

Note that move 5 **states the outcome up front**. It does not withhold. This is
the same finding as the retention research (`research/VIDEO_OPENINGS.md` §2):
a clear outcome is why staying matters. What Trust withholds is the
*mechanism*, never the *promise*.

### Scored against ours

§01 v8 opened on *"Three hundred and twenty billion."* — move 1 without moves
2–5: a number, no contradiction, no inversion onto the viewer, no promise.

Our move 3 is available and unused: **another model, also about five percent
active, fits one card where ours needs four.** That is the "Meanwhile: it's
2017" of this video, and it is the only thing in the field nobody else is
saying.

## 2. The abstraction is earned, never presented

Verbatim, the first interaction:

> **THE GAME OF TRUST**
>
> You have one choice. In front of you is a machine: if you put a coin in the
> machine, the other player gets three coins – and vice versa. You both can
> either choose to COOPERATE (put in coin), or CHEAT (don't put in coin).
>
> Let's say the other player cheats, and doesn't put in a coin. What should you
> do?

A payoff matrix is never introduced. It is *assembled* out of a machine the
reader has already operated, and by the time the grid of numbers appears every
cell is a memory.

### The rule

> **Build the abstraction out of a concrete thing the learner has already
> operated. Formal notation is a summary of their experience, not an
> introduction to it.**

Independently corroborated by Grant Sanderson, whose ODSC India 2019 talk is
titled *Concrete before Abstract*: *"resist the temptation to open a topic by
describing a general result or definition,"* and instead *"let examples precede
generality"* — finding the example that *"guides the audience to rediscover the
general results for themselves."*
(https://confengine.com/conferences/odsc-india-2019/proposal/10273/concrete-before-abstract)

We obey this in §05–06. We break it in §01, where *parameter*, *expert* and
*active* all arrive as definitions inside twenty seconds.

## 3. Mechanisms are given faces

Strategies are a cast with names and drawn faces: Copycat, Always Cooperate,
Always Cheat, Grudger, Detective, Simpleton, Copykitten.

Once a strategy is a person, *"what happens when Simpleton meets Always
Cooperate?"* is a question a reader can hold in their head. The source shows
exactly that being cashed in:

> Simpleton wins! This is because Simpleton is actually capable of exploiting
> Always Cooperate. They both start cooperating, but if Simpleton makes a
> mistake and cheats, since Always Cooperate never retaliates, it'll keep
> cheating them.

That sentence is only readable because both nouns are characters.

### The rule

> **Give a recurring mechanism a face and a name, and the learner can reason
> about it without re-deriving it.**

Our 288 experts are anonymous boxes and the router is a desk. §05 asks the
viewer to track a relationship between two things with no identity.

## 4. Hard prediction is used **twice**, not constantly

This is the correction that matters most for us, and it is countable in the
source. Across the whole piece there are exactly **two** places where the
reader commits to a prediction and the text branches on whether they were
right — `noise_evo_2_2` and `noise_evo_4_2`. The branch strings are:

> **"You were correct --"** / **"Your bet was close, but no cigar --"**

Two. In roughly twenty minutes of material. Case's own design-pattern post
warns *"do not mistake clicking for thinking"*
(`NCASE_4_MORE_DESIGN_PATTERNS.md`), and the artefact is disciplined about it:
prediction is spent where the answer is genuinely counterintuitive, and nowhere
else.

### The rule

> **Place Your Bets is a scarce resource. Two or three in a long piece, placed
> only where the correct answer is surprising. A guess the viewer can get right
> is worse than no guess, because it teaches them their model is fine.**

## 5. The reversal arrives late and takes back a belief

Verbatim, opening the noise chapter:

> As cool as Copycat is, it has a huge, fatal weakness I haven't mentioned yet.

Copycat has been winning for the preceding chapters. The reader has a settled
belief. Then miscommunication is introduced and Copycat loses to Copykitten.
The reversal is roughly two-thirds through.

### The rule

> **Place one conclusion-reversing surprise around two-thirds through, and make
> sure it reverses something the piece itself taught the viewer to believe.**

Ours is §12 — caching works, but not at 12,096 slots — and it is in the right
place. It is not currently *staged* as the taking-back of a belief, because
nothing earlier teaches the viewer to believe caching will save them.

## 6. It closes by turning the opening image into a metaphor

The outro, verbatim:

> If there's one big takeaway from all of game theory, it's this: **What the
> game is, defines what the players do.** […] In the short run, the game
> defines the players. But in the long run, it's us players who define the
> game. So, do what you can do, to create the conditions necessary to evolve
> trust. Build relationships. Find win-wins. Communicate clearly. Maybe then,
> we can stop firing at each other, get out of our own trenches, cross No Man's
> Land to come together...
>
> ...to live and let live.

It does not merely show the opening picture again. **It reuses the opening's
own nouns — "trenches", "No Man's Land" — as metaphors for the reader's life.**
The literal fact of move 1 becomes the figurative close. And the thesis is one
sentence, stated once, at the end: *what the game is, defines what the players
do.*

### The rule

> **Close by reusing the opening's concrete nouns figuratively, and state the
> thesis exactly once, in one sentence, at the end.**

We have nothing that returns, and our thesis — *"active parameters is a compute
number, not a memory number"* — is never said as one sentence.

## 7. What it does *not* do

- **No progress bar, no chapter titles, no "Part 3 of 7".** Structure is
  carried by the mechanic changing and the narration saying so.
- **No recap sections.** Earlier mechanics are still on screen and still
  working, so nothing needs re-explaining.
- **No hedging in the main path.** Caveats live in the conclusion.
- **Nothing is clickable that is not a thinking act.**
